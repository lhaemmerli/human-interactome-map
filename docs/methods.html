Methods, data sources and how to cite

The Human Interactome Map is an interactive view of known physical protein–protein interactions in humans. This page documents where every number on the site comes from, how it was computed, and what it cannot tell you.

2. Network and layout

Interactions are taken from STRING's physical sub-network, which only contains evidence that the two proteins bind (directly or in the same complex), excluding co-expression or text-mining-only links. Only pairs with a combined score ≥ 700 ("high confidence") are kept: 10,746 proteins and 86,519 interactions. Proteins without any such interaction are not shown.

Distance from the centre encodes connectivity: proteins are ranked by number of partners (degree) and placed at radius R·√(rank/N), so each ring holds the same area and the most connected proteins are central. Angle encodes modules: the network is split into communities with the Louvain algorithm (weighted by confidence), each community gets a sector proportional to its size, and positions inside a sector are ordered so that interacting proteins stay close. Sector labels show the most over-represented function and the largest hub of the module.

3. Annotations

  • Function: 22 classes built from UniProt keywords and Human Protein Atlas protein classes (e.g. "Kinase" = keyword Kinase). A protein can have several classes.
  • Body system: tissues in which the Human Protein Atlas reports the gene as enriched, group enriched or enhanced (RNA), mapped to 10 organ systems; genes with low tissue specificity are "Ubiquitous". Immune-cell-enriched genes also count as "Immune & blood".
  • Disease: Human Protein Atlas "disease involvement" terms (UniProt disease keywords and cancer-related genes), grouped into 25 categories. These are mostly genes with a disease-causing variant (Mendelian disease) or cancer genes; genes with small risk effects from population studies are largely absent.
  • Pathways: Reactome membership at all levels of the hierarchy (a protein in a specific pathway also belongs to its parents). 2,078 pathways with at least 5 proteins on the map are offered.
  • Domains: Pfam families from UniProt cross-references (99% of proteins on the map have at least one).

4. Statistics

  • Over-representation (partners' common domains, gene-list functions / systems / diseases / pathways / domains): one-sided hypergeometric test against all proteins on the map (for domains: all proteins with at least one Pfam domain), corrected for multiple testing with Benjamini–Hochberg; results with FDR q < 0.05 and at least 2 proteins are shown. "× vs all" is the fold enrichment (share in the set ÷ share in the background).
  • Connectivity of a gene list: the number of interactions inside the list is compared with the expectation for random proteins with the same numbers of partners (configuration model: E = (S² − Σdᵢ²) / 4m, with S the sum of the list's degrees and m the number of interactions). The p-value is the Poisson upper tail P(X ≥ observed).
  • Linker proteins: proteins outside the list linked to ≥ 2 list members, ranked by a hypergeometric p-value that accounts for how many partners they have overall.
  • Paths: all shortest paths (fewest interactions) are found by breadth-first search; among them, those with the most steps of documented effect in the path's direction come first, then the highest product of STRING confidences. Optionally, intermediate proteins can be restricted to selected Reactome pathways. When every step has a documented effect, the net effect is computed from the number of inhibitions (even = activation, odd = inhibition).
  • Evidence per interaction: STRING's channel scores for the physical network (experiments, curated databases, text mining) are shown for every link. BioGRID physical interactions between the same two human proteins (matched by UniProt accession) give the experimental methods, the number of low- and high-throughput publications, and up to 25 PubMed IDs (focused studies first, most recent first). Links with STRING experiments < 0.15, no curated-database score and no BioGRID record (4,545, 5.3%) are flagged as text mining only; the path finder ignores them by default.
  • Neighbourhood view: the selected protein, its most confident partners (and optionally the 3 strongest partners of its 10 closest partners), and all links among them, laid out with a force-directed simulation (springs on links, repulsion between proteins).
  • Activation / inhibition: OmniPath interactions (academic licence set) whose two proteins are linked on the map are kept when directed and signed. The consensus sign is used when the sources agree; when they disagree the effect is marked context-dependent. This covers 4,716 of the 86,519 interactions (5.5%): 4,111 activations, 1,012 inhibitions and 82 context-dependent effects (counted per direction).

5. Structures, interfaces, mutations and drugs

  • Single proteins: the AlphaFold DB model (coloured by pLDDT confidence) or the best experimental PDB chain according to PDBe's ranking (coverage, then resolution).
  • Binding interfaces: a pair of interacting proteins is "explained" when one Pfam domain of each is listed in 3did as being in contact in at least one PDB structure, between different chains (intramolecular contacts are excluded). About 26% of interactions are explained this way. This is an inference: the two domain types are known to bind, not necessarily in this exact protein pair.
  • Complexes: a solved structure containing both proteins is searched by intersecting their PDBe structure lists; interface residues are those with a heavy atom within 4.5 Å of the other chain. If none exists, the 3did example for the domain pair is shown and labelled as such.
  • Disease mutations: UniProt humsavar missense variants classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic (27,002 variants in 2,687 proteins on the map), drawn at their residue on the AlphaFold model (same numbering as the UniProt canonical sequence).
  • Mutation sensitivity: the mean AlphaMissense pathogenicity of the 19 possible substitutions at each residue (Cheng et al., Science 2023).
  • Drugs: Open Targets "drug and clinical candidates" for the protein's Ensembl gene, with the highest clinical stage reached and the mechanism of action on this target.

6. Limitations

  • Study bias: well-studied proteins (e.g. TP53, ubiquitin, histones) have more recorded interactions; being a hub partly reflects research attention, not only biology.
  • Context: an interaction in the database means the two proteins can bind, not that they do so in every tissue, cell type or condition.
  • Thresholds: the 700 cut-off removes weaker evidence; some real interactions are missing and some false positives remain.
  • Effects are partial and contextual: about 95% of interactions have no documented effect (they are shown as plain "binds" links), and a documented activation or inhibition may only hold in some cells or conditions.
  • Text-mining links: STRING's physical network also contains links supported only by co-mention in papers. They are flagged rather than removed, because some reflect real but not-yet-curated interactions.
  • Shortest ≠ biological: the shortest path can skip the canonical signalling route; use the pathway restriction to follow known pathways.
  • Annotations are incomplete: about 2,100 proteins have no function class and some have no tissue data; protein descriptions are shown in English (UniProt).
  • Not medical advice: this is an educational and exploratory tool.

7. How to cite

If you use this map or its processed data, please cite the website and the underlying resources (at least STRING, UniProt and the Human Protein Atlas):

Haemmerli, L. (2026). Human Interactome Map [Interactive web resource]. https://lhaemmerli.github.io/human-interactome-map/
@misc{haemmerli2026interactome,
  author       = {Haemmerli, Leo},
  title        = {Human Interactome Map},
  year         = {2026},
  howpublished = {\url{https://lhaemmerli.github.io/human-interactome-map/}},
  note         = {Data: STRING v12.0, UniProt 2026\_04, Human Protein Atlas, Reactome v98}
}
