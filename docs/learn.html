What is this website?
A map of the tiny machines inside you.

Your body is built and run by proteins: about 20,000 different kinds. They almost never work alone. This page explains, with things you can click and drag, what a protein is and why a map of how they connect is useful.

1A protein is a chain that folds

A protein is a long chain of small building blocks called amino acids, like beads on a string. There are 20 kinds of beads. Your DNA is the recipe that says in which order to string them.

Hover or tap a bead to see which amino acid it is.
avoids water (hides inside) likes water positive charge negative charge
This is a real piece of insulin (24 amino acids).
Once the chain is made, it folds on its own into a precise shape: the water-avoiding beads tuck inside, the water-loving ones stay outside. The shape decides what the protein can do, the way a key's shape decides which lock it opens.

4Proteins work in teams

To do their jobs, proteins grab onto each other. Two proteins whose surfaces fit together can stick, like puzzle pieces. That contact is called a protein–protein interaction.

The map you are about to explore contains 86,519 such handshakes between 10,746 human proteins, collected from thousands of experiments.

5Hubs: the proteins everyone talks to

Draw every interaction as a line and you get a network. Most proteins have few partners, but a few hubs have hundreds. Click proteins below to remove them and watch what happens to the team.

Tip: try the big one in the middle.
Losing a hub can break the whole team, which is why hub proteins are often essential and often linked to disease. On the map, hubs sit at the centre.

6When one letter changes

A mutation is a typo in the recipe. Sometimes one wrong amino acid changes the shape, and the protein stops working or sticks to the wrong partners.

This is sickle-cell anaemia: the changed haemoglobin molecules stick to each other, form long fibres and bend the red blood cell into a sickle. On the map, the Disease filter shows which proteins are linked to illnesses like this one, cancer, diabetes or Alzheimer's.
